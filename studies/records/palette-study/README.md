# Color identity study

Five original proposals on the approved **B / #FAF6EE** plot background, with **Helvetica Regular**, 0.55 pt black frames and 1.15 pt curves. The approved theme remains the default.

[Browse and switch all six plots](gallery.html) · [Research and visual comparison](palette-study.pdf) · [Quick comparison](comparison.png)

## Recommendation

**Start with P2 Aubergine & Brass.** It provides a more distinctive signature while staying quiet enough for scientific figures. P3 Oxide & Denim is the warmest alternative; P5 Ink & Persimmon has more graphic energy. P1 is the conservative all-purpose choice. P4 is the closest evolution of the current style.

“Distinctive” is a design judgment. This research did not measure palette prevalence across scientific publications or establish that these colors are unique. The combinations are newly selected; no complete brand palette was copied.

## What the research changes

Use a signature hue consistently, two companion hues for everyday comparisons, and a neutral for context. Keep category identity separate from sequential or diverging magnitude scales. Expanded palettes are useful in dense bar examples, but permanent scientific figures should favor direct labels, a smaller active set, or a signature-plus-neutral view.

The six source figures retain their arrays, uncertainty values, axes, geometry and ordering. The line and radar samples add distinct marker shapes; line samples also distinguish dash patterns. The source bar examples still have their original truncated y axes. The profile pages add a directly labeled AUROC point-and-interval view using those same supplied uncertainty values. Only the explicitly labeled heatmaps use synthetic data.

## Exact palette definitions

First color = signature; next two = companions. Colors 4–6 extend the set. Hex values are sRGB. Keep text #282828, secondary text #666666, grid #E3DCCF, border #000000, margins #FFFFFF. Font and size rules remain in the [approved style guide](../README.md).

## Assignment rules

- Three methods: DPO = companion 1 / square / dashed; DA-DPO = companion 2 / triangle / dash-dot; VIGIL = signature / circle / solid. Radar retains solid lines with distinct shapes.
- Two trend categories: evaluation/application = companion 1; methodological contribution = signature. Original fill opacity is retained.
- Nine-method bars: extra 1, color 6, extra 2, color 4, color 2, color 5, extra 3, color 3, signature. Seven-method composition: colors 1–6, then extra 1. Complete category-to-hex assignments are in palettes.json.
- Prefer the first three colors for thin lines. The extended set is a candidate vocabulary, not nine equally distinguishable series under every viewing condition.
- A category keeps its color across figures; add explicit labels and shapes. Do not assign a color purely by its position in a changing sort order.
- Sequential ramps represent magnitude. Diverging ramps require a meaningful midpoint; use symmetric limits for signed comparisons. The missing-data color should be separately explained, never silently conflated with zero.

## Checks and limits

All 45 proposed opaque category colors exceed 3:1 against the cream background. This is a design screen, not full WCAG certification; contrast to adjacent data colors and opacity also matter. Detailed numbers, close-pair flags and monotonic ramp checks are in [validation.json](validation.json).

The gallery shows Machado severity-100 protan/deutan/tritan simulations and grayscale on the actual plots. These are approximations, not user testing or a guarantee of accessibility. Oklab distances below 0.06 are flagged for review as an editorial heuristic, not an accepted accessibility threshold. Grayscale collapses some categories in every palette; labels, positions, marker shapes and line styles carry the remaining distinctions.

Sequential and diverging ramps are interpolated in Oklab, with chroma reduced to stay within sRGB. Monotonic luminance is checked at 257 samples. This alone does not establish perceptual uniformity or equal discriminability under all color-vision conditions. For sensitive quantitative imaging, prefer a validated scientific colormap and retain the house identity in labels and categorical marks.

Submission settings remain journal-specific. Nature currently asks for ticks, discourages grids and patterns, and specifies different final text sizes. The cream/grid/hatch house style is therefore not a claim of automatic Nature submission compliance. RGB-to-CMYK print proofs may also change color appearance. [Nature specifications](https://research-figure-guide.nature.com/figures/preparing-figures-our-specifications/)

## Research notes

| Source | Published practice | Adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| [The Economist: choosing a limited chart palette](https://education.economist.com/blog/interviews/tips-for-visualising-data-like-the-economist) | The team describes dark and light blue as recurring colors, a small working palette, and highlighting a main series when many series compete. | Use one recognizable signature color repeatedly, with two companions and a quiet neutral. |
| [BBC Visual and Data Journalism: R graphics cookbook](https://bbc.github.io/rcookbook/) | The public cookbook separates its reusable chart theme from explicitly assigned data colors. Examples use blue #1380A1 and other deliberate color assignments. | Keep typography and framing fixed while changing named color roles. The cookbook is a 2019 public reference, not a claim about every current BBC graphic. |
| [ONS: using colours in charts](https://service-manual.ons.gov.uk/data-visualisation/colours/using-colours-in-charts) | The palette includes ocean blue #206095, spring green #A8BD3A and beetroot #871A5B. Guidance favors few categories, consistent mappings, and gray context plus a highlighted series. | Separate an everyday three-color core from an extended palette. Many categories should not automatically mean many equally prominent colors. |
| [Datawrapper: colors in data-vis style guides](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/colors-for-data-vis-style-guides) | The guide distinguishes categories, accents, neutral grays, and sequential/diverging scales. Its discussion with the Financial Times explains how a tinted background constrains usable lightness and neutrals. | Specify a complete system of roles and test colors on the actual cream background, not isolated white swatches. |
| [Nature: preparing figures, our specifications](https://research-figure-guide.nature.com/figures/preparing-figures-our-specifications/) | The research-figure guide asks for accessible colors, standard sans-serif fonts such as Helvetica, and dark legend text. It also has its own rules for ticks, grids, patterns and final text size. | Use the current style as a lab identity, then apply journal-specific output settings at submission. A familiar journal name does not define one universal author-figure palette. |
| [ACS Inclusivity Style Guide: data visualization](https://www.acs.org/about/inclusion/inclusivity-style-guide/data-visualization.html) | ACS recommends considering color-vision deficiency and grayscale, matching color-scale type to data, and combining color with direct labels or symbols. | Use redundant markers and line patterns, and keep categorical identity separate from numerical magnitude. |
| [Fabio Crameri: Scientific colour maps](https://www.fabiocrameri.ch/colourmaps/) | The Scientific colour maps project develops ordered, perceptually controlled scales for quantitative data, including color-vision and grayscale considerations. | Treat continuous scales as a separate engineering problem. The custom ramps here are prototypes, not replacements for validated scientific maps in demanding measurements. |
| [Les Couleurs: Le Corbusier color system](https://www.lescouleurs.ch/the-colour-system) | The architectural system contains 63 colors in two collections. It organizes solid colors and graduated shades, and uses color keyboards to explore combinations. | Borrow the relationship of muted mineral hues, warm neutrals and a few stronger accents. No official swatch set or color codes were reproduced. |
| [Albers Foundation: Interaction of Color](https://www.albersfoundation.org/alberses/teaching/interaction-of-color) | Albers's teaching examines how neighboring colors and context change perceived color through practical visual exercises. | Judge every palette in thin curves, large fills and actual plots on the approved background. This informs the method, not a borrowed Albers palette. |
| [Albers Foundation: In Thread and On Paper](https://www.albersfoundation.org/exhibitions/anni-albers-in-thread-and-on-paper) | The exhibition documents movement between woven textiles, prints and functional design, including cotton samples and wool works. | Use material and dye associations as creative prompts for P2 and P3. The proposed hex values are new selections, not measured colors from these works. |
| [Martian Press: ink colors](https://martian.press/ink-colors) | The studio works with a finite ink library. It explains that largely transparent inks, layering and paper affect the resulting color; digital swatches are approximations. | Borrow the limited-ink discipline for P5. Avoid fluorescence, paper texture and overprint effects in quantitative marks; none of its ink palette is copied wholesale. |
| [W3C: understanding non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html) | The guidance uses a 3:1 contrast criterion for meaningful graphical elements against adjacent colors and notes that thin anti-aliased marks can appear fainter. | Check opaque series against cream at 3:1 as a screening target. This is not a claim that every complete figure is WCAG compliant. |
| [Bjorn Ottosson: Oklab color space](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/) | Oklab represents lightness and two chromatic coordinates and supports perceptual color interpolation. | Build prototype ramps with controlled lightness, then inspect them and test for monotonic luminance after conversion to sRGB. |
| [Machado et al. matrices in Colorspacious](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/njsmith/colorspacious/master/colorspacious/cvd.py) | This implementation distributes the 2009 Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes simulation matrices over a range of severities. | Use severity-100 protan, deutan and tritan matrices on linear RGB, plus luminance grayscale. Simulations are screening aids, not guarantees of accessibility. |
| [Nature Reviews: figure and illustration guide](https://www.nature.com/documents/natrev-artworkguide.pdf) | For commissioned explanatory illustrations, Nature organizes its house palette into accent colors and neutral context, with an extended palette for grouping and exceptions for scientific conventions. | Borrow the hierarchy of focus versus context. Distinguish this editorial illustration system from the research-figure specifications; neither is a palette to clone. |
| [Okabe and Ito: Color Universal Design](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/) | The authors propose blue, sky blue, bluish green, vermilion, orange, yellow and reddish purple with black, and recommend direct labeling and redundant shape/line coding. | Use this as a functional reference. Our muted material palettes serve identity and require their own checks; they do not inherit the accessibility of another palette. |

## Reproduce

From the repository root, using the existing requirements:

```sh
python figures4papers-minimal/render_palette_study.py
```

Definitions: `../palette_schemes.py`. Rendering and temporary palette context: `../render_palette_study.py`. Color math: `../palette_color_math.py`. The approved default is not switched by running the study. No new runtime dependencies are required.
